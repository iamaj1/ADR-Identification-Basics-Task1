# ADR Identification Basics — Task 1

## Task Objective

Analyze the patient case to identify a potential Adverse Drug Reaction (ADR), review the medication history and timeline, and identify the suspected drug.

## Patient Scenario

- Patient: Female, 45 years
- Symptoms: Rash, itching, nausea, and dizziness
- Medication: Amoxicillin (started 5 days before symptoms); Paracetamol as needed
- Timeline: Amoxicillin was started → symptoms appeared → symptoms persisted.

## ADR Assessment

- ADR suspected: Yes
- Suspected drug: Amoxicillin
- Suspected adverse reactions: Rash, itching, nausea, dizziness
- Drug-event relationship: Possible / suspected drug-related reaction

## Reasoning

The symptoms appeared after initiation of amoxicillin and persisted afterward. The temporal relationship supports suspicion of an ADR. Causality is classified as possible/suspected rather than confirmed because the available case information alone does not establish definitive causality.

## Conclusion

The case represents a suspected adverse drug reaction associated with amoxicillin. Further clinical assessment would be required for definitive causality assessment.

## Final Result

Suspected ADR: Yes

Suspected Drug: Amoxicillin

Adverse Reactions:
- Rash
- Itching
- Nausea
- Dizziness

Conclusion: Possible / suspected adverse drug reaction associated with Amoxicillin based on the temporal relationship.
